## Conventions and mathematical care

- Geometric variables count trials through the first success, with support starting at one. Failure counts are explicitly defined as the shifted variable. OpenStax §4.4 contains inconsistent surrounding prose suggesting changed standard deviation across the conventions; the displayed formulas are algebraically equal. The authored lesson independently derives identical variance under the shift and distinguishes the means by one.
- Bernoulli and binomial endpoint probabilities zero and one are handled as deterministic cases, extending Pishro-Nik's interior-parameter convention explicitly. The geometric endpoint one is deterministic; parameter zero does not yield a finite-valued first-success waiting time.
- Hypergeometric parameters are `(N,K,n)` for total population, marked count, and sample size. Pishro-Nik instead supplies marked and unmarked population counts separately. The translation, feasible support, full sample, one-object population, and empty-population boundaries are stated. The variance correction is derived in the lesson from ordered marked pairs and the second-moment identity, not attributed to a formula absent from the pinpoint PMF passage.
- Finite expectations and moment identities are stated with integrability/finite-second-moment conditions. A telescoping normalized PMF with an infinite harmonic mean demonstrates why a valid PMF need not have a finite expectation. This is an original series calculation using the existing calculus prerequisite; no claim is made that the reference contains that example.
- A probability-zero conditioning event is treated as undefined under the elementary ratio, not equated with the empty event. The zero-prior Bayes task uses monotonicity of the joint event rather than an undefined conditional likelihood times zero.
- Prior odds and likelihood ratios are derived before their required computational use. Early base-rate feedback avoids invoking odds before that section.
- Poisson examples state the count model and observation interval. Rates alone are not asserted to imply a Poisson distribution. Independent increments apply to disjoint intervals; doubling a count is distinguished from adding independent copies.

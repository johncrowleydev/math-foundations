# Final curriculum prerequisite and progression audit

This audit follows the learning sequence across all 71 instructional lessons and four introductions. It is separate from Review activation and scheduling.

The complete per-lesson inventories are:

- [Discrete Mathematics and Linear Algebra](audits/final-discrete-linear-prerequisites.md): 27 lessons.
- [Calculus and Probability & Statistics](audits/final-calculus-probability-prerequisites.md): 44 lessons.

Each inventory classifies dependencies as **intentional prerequisite**, **local refresher exists**, **acceptable common algebra**, **sequencing concern**, or **missing prerequisite support**, and records exercise progression. The absence of a concern is reported explicitly; classifications are not quotas.

No missing prerequisite unit, mandatory sequencing change or clear instructional difficulty cliff was found along the declared complete curriculum path with its stated entry preparation. Algebra, coordinate geometry, exponents/logarithms and elementary trigonometry are intentional preparation, with local reminders where used. This curriculum does not promise to teach all precalculus from scratch.

| Transition                            | Dependency assessment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Entry → Discrete Mathematics          | Number sets, ordinary equations/inequalities, powers and algebra are entry preparation. Logical connectives, quantifier scope, set/function notation, summations and proof methods are introduced locally before their required use. Early matrix/graph representations in Relations include their own explanation. |
| Discrete Mathematics → Linear Algebra | Functions, sets, composition and proof habits are available. Vectors, dot products and matrix shapes are introduced before systems, span and bases. Bases precede rank/inverses; projections precede least squares. Least squares uses projection geometry and does not silently require calculus.                  |
| Linear Algebra → Calculus             | Elementary function/algebra/trigonometric fluency is stated preparation. The single-variable sequence builds limits, derivatives and accumulation before series and multivariable work. Vectors, dot products, matrices and eigenvalue ideas support gradients, Jacobians and Hessians.                             |
| Calculus → Probability & Statistics   | Counting, sets and sums support finite probability; integration supports continuous densities and moments; derivatives and optimization support likelihood; vector/matrix and least-squares ideas support covariance and regression. The order places these prerequisites before their statistical applications.    |

The concrete progression defect found during the mathematical pass was **missing local setup in independently selectable exercises and Review variants**: prompts such as “that approximation” or “the same motion” depended on an earlier question that focused practice need not show. Repairs restate the necessary data in place. They do not add a new topic or rearrange the curriculum.

Optional practice contains repeated numerical scaffolding and longer proof/application work. It is not all compulsory in one reading session. The subject report records two LOW repetition concerns in Calculus 16 and 19; neither justified changing the banks during this pass. The audit found no reason to equalize exercise or Review-template counts, remove productive deep work, or manufacture transfer cards solely for numerical balance. These are inspection findings, not empirical measurements of learner difficulty or retention.

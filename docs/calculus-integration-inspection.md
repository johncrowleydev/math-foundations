## Mathematical verification

A separate temporary Python environment used SymPy 1.14.0 for independent calculations. It did not use the app's grading engine or regenerate expected answers from the authoring helpers.

- Independently differentiated all **52 authored antiderivative answers**, including the practice and review initial-value examples. Checked arbitrary constants by differentiating with respect to C and evaluated each supplied initial condition separately. The scaled inverse-sine cases were simplified on their explicitly stated radical domains.
- Independently evaluated **16 selected definite integrals and application models**. These include horizontal area 9/2; square-slice volume 26/3; rotation about an offset axis giving 5π/3; spring work 3 J; logarithmic substitution 1/2; a parts integral equal to 1; rational division giving (1−ln 2)/2; infinite-tail and endpoint-singularity values; trigonometric cubes; and an exponential normalization.
- Recomputed midpoint, trapezoidal, and Simpson estimates for x² on [0,2] directly from their sample points, obtaining **5/2, 3, and 8/3** respectively.
- Independently computed **five cancellation limits**, **five Taylor coefficient expansions**, and **four infinite sums**. This included the third-order sine cancellation, fourth-order cosine cancellation, a nonzero-center square-root polynomial, and telescoping sums with shifted indices.
- Direct rational arithmetic checked the Taylor integral approximation **443/960** and its first omitted term bound **1/5376**, sine approximation **23/48** and error **1/3840**, and the distinction between absolute and relative error in the numerical examples.
- Separately checked the sign/order arguments in the open tasks: ordinary improper integration cannot use symmetric cancellation; term limits alone do not establish series convergence; decreasing alternating magnitudes are required; comparison directions are not reversible; and convergence intervals require both endpoint checks.

These computations are a representative independent mathematical pass plus complete differentiation of the authored antiderivative fixtures. They do not claim that every open explanation has been machine-proved or that every numerical exercise was independently recomputed in software.

## Integration checks and boundaries

The modules contain **360 practice questions, 108 dedicated review questions, and 14 quick checks** across seven lessons. Review questions are newly authored in each teaching section. Proofs, construction tasks, modeling judgments, and extended explanations remain open responses.

A temporary harness parses every authored math fragment with KaTeX, rejects control characters from accidental JavaScript escapes, verifies exactly two quick checks per lesson, and exercises every deterministic answer fixture with a correct and incorrect response. The shared helper routes constant expressions involving π, e, or named functions to the calculus-expression validator with no variables; rational numeric answers use the existing exact validator.

The first fixture pass identified four correct answers not recognized by the new engine: the sine-square and cosine-square antiderivatives requiring double-angle normalization, and two scaled inverse-sine antiderivatives requiring positive rational square-root scaling. These were reported to the grading implementation agent rather than weakening their mathematical answers. Explicit equivalent radicand positivity facts are supplied in those two inverse-sine requirements. After the engine correction in commit 50c34d1, all **738 correct/incorrect fixture evaluations passed**, along with the complete KaTeX and control-character checks.

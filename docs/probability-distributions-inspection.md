## Conventions and deliberate qualifications

- These lessons use `N(mean, variance)`, matching Pishro-Nik. OpenStax uses `N(mean, standard deviation)`, including its [§7.1 sample-mean notation](https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/7-1-the-central-limit-theorem-for-sample-means-averages), inspected for comparison. Lesson 10 explicitly identifies the second argument and demonstrates taking its square root. This notation difference must be retained when displaying source details or adding exercises.
- Exponential parameters are rates. Waiting-time quantities include their units, and total elapsed time is distinguished from additional waiting time.
- The continuous-density block deliberately excludes singular continuous distributions. Transformations can still produce mixed distributions; the lesson explicitly accounts for atoms rather than asserting every transformation has a density.
- Conditional-density formulas require a positive marginal density at the specified conditioning value. Density equalities relevant to independence are understood up to sets of measure zero. A formula's apparent factorization inside a triangular support is insufficient for independence.
- Pishro-Nik's random-vector section also calls the uncentered matrix `E[XX^T]` a correlation matrix. These lessons use covariance matrices and standardized scalar correlations; they do not adopt that uncentered terminology.
- Random assignment balances covariates in distribution, not exactly in every finite experiment. Our study-design text avoids reading OpenStax's informal balancing/causation language as an automatic guarantee. Random sampling and random assignment answer different questions.
- The weak-law statement uses iid integrable variables; its elementary quantitative proof additionally assumes finite variance. The stated classical CLT requires iid variables with finite positive variance. A sample size of thirty is not taught as a theorem or universal adequacy threshold. Exact normal averages are a separate result.
- Quantile computations use strictly increasing CDF portions. The general smallest-location convention is stated explicitly; it is not silently equated with every empirical percentile interpolation convention.

## Independent mathematical checks

A temporary SymPy/Python calculation script completed 110 independent exact, integral, enumeration, matrix, and numerical-table checks. It did not import the authoring helpers or derive expected answers from the grading fixtures. Representative results and methods:

- Integrated four polynomial densities directly to check normalization, means, raw second moments, and centered variance. Separately evaluated the interval probability `13/32`, truncated probability `19/56`, and inverse CDF value `3/2`.
- Checked improper moments: density `2/x^3` on `x>1` has unit mass, mean `2`, and infinite second moment; density `4/x^5` has second moment `2`. Recomputed four uniform variances and exponential normalization/first/second moments for four rates.
- Differentiated transformed CDFs and integrated the resulting densities for a square, reciprocal, and exponential transformation. Direct integrals checked square mean `4/3`, exponential mean `(e^2-1)/2`, clipped mean `1/4`, and capped mean `3/4`.
- Enumerated joint-table cells for events, marginals, and conditional values. Independently integrated the joint density `4xy` over rectangle and diagonal regions, obtaining `1/16` and `1/6`, and checked its product moment `4/9`. Integrated triangular regions for marginal densities and conditional event results `4/7`, `3/8`, and `1/3`.
- Enumerated binary covariance `1/8` and a dependent but uncorrelated pair `X,X^2`; checked `Var(X^2)=2/9`. Direct matrix multiplication verified sum/difference variances, a nontrivial affine covariance matrix `[[5,-3],[-3,5]]`, eigenvalue signs, and a singular covariance determinant.
- Enumerated a four-point mixture to obtain mean `7/2` and variance `39/4` without using the total-variance formula. Direct integration for joint density `1/(2x)` on `0<y<x<2` gave mean `1/2` and variance `7/36` for `Y`.
- Enumerated an independent random-count model separately from the symbolic random-sum derivation, obtaining mean `15` and variance `56`. Checked conditional binary prediction risks `21/100` and `1/4` directly.
- Recomputed three sample means/variances, three pooled or frequency-weighted summaries, and a Bernoulli sample-mean distribution by enumeration. Checked four Chebyshev bounds, three standard errors, and two shared-noise average variances.
- Recomputed supplied standard-normal CDF entries at `0.5,1,1.1,1.5,2` through the error function; all agree with the supplied four-decimal values within `0.00005`. Exercises include the needed table entries and rounding instruction, so no runtime statistical calculator is required.

These checks supplement, rather than replace, reading the theorem hypotheses and worked reasoning. They are not a claim that every open response has been mechanically proved.

# Linear Algebra review coverage audit

This audit inspected all 12 instructional lessons, their 51 authored teaching concepts, and **151 distinct effective concept × skill × optional objective targets** before authoring. The baseline came from the server’s Review Library catalog, with lesson exercises already filtered by evidence compatibility. It contained 788 effective templates: 645 production, 103 reasoning, and 40 recognition; all were reused lesson exercises, and 40 were Quick-compatible. There were no dedicated Linear Algebra review templates and no recall objectives.

A reused exercise can represent several targets; these are not counts of unique questions. There is no coverage or mastery score. No longitudinal Linear Algebra learner evidence was available, so additions are conservative authoring judgments.

## The actual depth gap

Exercises `linear-algebra-projections-49`, `-60`, `-63`, `-64`, and `-65` activate `fit-interpretation`, but their current deterministic assessments provide recognition while their primary skills request evaluation, analysis, or justification. The scheduler correctly filters these shallower representations out. Reading the source exercises alone would incorrectly suggest that model interpretation already had effective review.

The new `fit-interpretation × justify` family asks for reasons, not choice selection. One variant compares two rules agreeing on training observations but disagreeing on a new input; the other computes and explains how scaling one observation changes squared-error weighting. A separate `fit-interpretation × interpret × la-fit-scope-recognition` family supplies two Quick probes. The cheap objective cannot satisfy the reasoning target. Existing lesson assessments and their historical evidence are unchanged.

## Added representations and activation

The additions are **41 dedicated templates: 30 fixed terminology probes and 11 authored families with 22 variants**. Nine authored families provide recognition (18 variants); two provide reasoning (four variants). There are no new generators or validator families. Thirty-nine added templates are Quick-compatible: the 30 short-text term responses and nine tap-based recognition families. The two reasoning families remain Regular-only.

The fixed term probes use the existing deterministic `term` validator, with 30 fixture rows covering accepted terms, case/whitespace normalization, a specific confusing term, explicit negation, and missing input. Naming a term is production evidence only for its own recall objective; it does not certify constructing a vector, computing an inverse, or proving independence. Related accepted terms are explicit, including kernel/null space and image/range. Exact repetition is intentional for precise vocabulary.

Every new objective activates from an existing concept with actual lesson exercises. None activates from reading. Kernel and null-space naming use their taught contexts, and the shared definitions explicitly acknowledge their equivalence. The fit-interpretation objectives activate from the existing fit-interpretation exercises. The cumulative family activates only from the later `least-squares` concept: activation concepts are alternatives, so adding earlier prerequisites to that list would incorrectly activate the task too early.

The independent server audit rebuilt the content, confirmed that all 41 additions survived effective-catalog filtering, confirmed 39 Quick-compatible additions, and previewed every added template. With the unchanged reused pool, this yields 829 Linear Algebra effective templates and 184 distinct targets (79 Quick-compatible templates). Final actual-lesson filter counts and browser inspection are recorded by the curriculum-wide integration audit.

## Cumulative review and subject boundaries

`la-fit-null-space-transfer-review` is one curated cumulative family with two variants. Learners find every least-squares coefficient vector, the closest output, and the residual, then connect unique projection geometry with nonunique coefficients differing by null-space vectors. One matrix has proportional columns spanning a coordinate axis; the other has equal columns spanning a diagonal line. These change the geometry and null-space relation. The existing least-squares proof establishes invertibility under independent columns and does not provide this transfer task.

Discrete-to-Linear bridges already have active function, proof, and summation targets; linear combination and matrix shape review make those concepts usable here. Linear-to-Calculus bridges already include vector operations, dot products, functions, coordinates, and matrix transformations with substantial production coverage. The least-squares cumulative family and fit-interpretation restoration also support the later regression boundary. No duplicate bridge deck or standalone algebra/precalculus subject is warranted.

## Intentional gaps and deferred work

- Most computational targets already have 6–21 reusable representations. Their empty authored-variant columns do not imply a single repeated exercise. Dedicated numerical generators would add maintenance without addressing the principal gaps found here.
- Single general proofs, including dot-product distributivity, projection optimality, and symmetric-eigenvector orthogonality, intentionally remain expensive reasoning. A one-template proof target is not automatically defective; prompt-specific repetition should be monitored in real delayed attempts.
- Column space, Euclidean norm, unit vector, orthonormality, eigenspace, diagonalization, QR/Gram–Schmidt, and truncation retain their existing calculation/recognition context. No term is given every possible retrieval direction. The most central exact names receive standalone retrieval first; other vocabulary can be refined after learner evidence.
- Choosing a correct independent list does not replace constructing or proving independence. Shape recognition does not replace matrix multiplication, and rectangular-SVD recognition does not replace reconstruction or reasoning about information loss.
- Full SVD algorithms, numerical-rank calibration, matrix conditioning algorithms, and proofs beyond the taught scope remain excluded. Statistical causal inference is left to the existing later subject.
- There is no assertion that these authored representations calibrate scheduling, establish mastery, or permanently complete the curriculum.

## Findings and decisions

**10 — Mathematical Induction.** The effective pool already includes 54 reasoning templates, including thirteen `induction-hypothesis × prove`, ten inequality proofs, ten recursive-definition proofs, four loop-invariant proofs, and three strengthened-induction proofs. The two productive hypothesis representations and ten Quick recognition/interpretation templates are retained. Missing vocabulary retrieval is filled with the induction hypothesis and loop invariant, plus three role variants distinguishing base case, the forward implication step, and finite numerical examples from a proof. Base-case and step roles belong to `induction-principle × recall × induction-proof-roles`; this recognition objective cannot clear the existing proof targets. No further induction proofs are warranted by this inventory.

**11 — Strong Induction.** All 24 existing target rows were inspected, including well-ordering, recursive correctness/termination, structural induction, prime-factorization existence, uniqueness, recursive definitions, and proof diagnosis. There are already eleven strong-induction proofs, nine recursive-correctness proofs, four structural-induction proofs, and seven Quick strong-hypothesis interpretation templates. Add exact retrieval of the term “strong induction” and two well-ordering/domain probes. They distinguish a least nonnegative integer from a largest element, and strict decrease in the positive reals from a well-founded integer measure. Existing productive construction and deeper proofs remain the primary evidence; the single structural counterexample is deliberately not padded with near-copies.

**12 — Combinatorics.** All twenty rows were inspected: sum/product rules, complementary counting, permutations/combinations, restrictions, uniform overcounts/symmetries, stars and bars, binomial coefficients/theorem, inclusion–exclusion, pigeonhole reasoning, and double counting. The baseline has 68 production and seven reasoning templates but only three Quick templates. New concise term retrieval covers permutation, combination and pigeonhole principle; inclusion–exclusion gets two overlap/definition directions. The counting-model family contrasts distinguishable roles, unordered committees, and unequal disjoint branches. A separate exact-answer family supplies three short production cases (independent positions, disjoint branches, ordered selection without replacement). Existing factorial/binomial computations, eleven counting-principle computations, six inclusion–exclusion computations, double-counting proofs, and pigeonhole proofs remain reusable. Stars-and-bars and restricted-counting Quick cells remain intentionally empty: these topics already have productive review, and no learner evidence yet justifies isolating every formula into another card.

**13 — Recurrence Relations.** All 33 rows were inspected: recursive definitions, unfolding/telescoping, affine and forced recurrences, characteristic roots, repeated roots, counting recurrences, verification, divide-and-conquer, and recurrence bounds. Initial conditions/recursive-definition vocabulary is handled at its earlier teaching location in lesson 6 by the companion audit, avoiding another copy here. Add characteristic-equation term retrieval, first-order/homogeneous distinctions, three short exact evaluations with different dependency/index structures, and two genuinely productive characteristic-polynomial variants. The latter supplement the single existing `characteristic-roots × represent` exercise and accept equivalent factored forms through the existing expression checker. They do not ask for a proof or pretend to certify one. Two existing verification proofs, the divide-and-conquer proof, and recurrence-bound proofs already combine induction with recurrences; no extra transfer proof duplicates them. Repeated-root derivations, counting constructions and recurrence growth reasoning remain deeper work.

**14 — Graph Theory.** All 36 rows were inspected, including graph conventions, degrees, routes, components, trees/forests, rooted trees, DAG orders, representations, BFS/DFS, Euler routes and Hamiltonian routes. Although 28 templates are already Quick, dedicated terminology is absent. Add vertex/edge roles, degree retrieval, walk/trail/path/cycle contrasts, component/connected distinctions, tree/forest distinctions, root/leaf roles, DAG expansion, and topological-order meaning. Three route-classification variants change the reasoning: repeated vertex without repeated edge, the same undirected edge in reverse, and a missing first edge. They use a separate recognition objective because the existing `graph-routes × recognize` target requires production. `dag-order × construct` previously relied on one five-vertex question; two compact three-vertex variants use the existing graph validator to require **every** valid ordering for a fork versus a chain. Enumerating orders stays production and Quick. Existing degree computations, tree/connectivity proofs, graph counterexamples, search procedures, Euler constructions and Hamiltonian constructions remain effective. Individual theorem-proof targets with one exercise are not automatically defects.

**15 — Asymptotic Growth and Algorithmic Reasoning.** All nineteen rows were inspected, including O/Omega/Theta, little-o, comparison rules, logarithmic/polynomial/exponential growth, loop counting, case complexity, cost models and lower-bound arguments. There are 27 reasoning templates and thirteen productive loop-counting templates. Add bound-direction retrieval, two fixed-constant/threshold probes, three dominant-growth comparisons, worst-case term retrieval, and exact-count versus growth distinctions. An upper bound alone does not earn a tight-bound conclusion; neither represents proof evidence. The single existing productive growth comparison remains intact. No general symbolic asymptotic grader or additional generator is introduced.

## Cumulative review, activation and subject bridges

One two-variant Regular reasoning family connects graphs to asymptotics. Learners derive the number of vertex/neighbor-entry operations for a tree versus a complete graph and justify their different tight growth. The results are `3n−2` and `n²`, respectively, under the explicitly stated unit-cost model. This tests whether the learner can use the degree sum and graph structure when analyzing apparently nested loops. It is not two unrelated questions concatenated together.

Activation uses the existing concepts. Most additions activate on their own taught concept. Recurrence evaluations use `recurrence-unfolding`, the later lesson-13 gate, although their scheduler target is shared `recursive-definition × compute`. The cumulative family activates only on `cost-model`, taught in lesson 15; adding `graph-degree` as an alternative would activate it before asymptotic notation is taught because activation concepts have OR semantics. No reading position is used for activation. A learner can still deliberately request focused practice, as intended by the existing system.

These lessons already provide recurrence/induction and counting/graph bridges. Summation notation, functions and proof language support the later Linear Algebra transition through their normal targets; this pass does not duplicate them or create a separate prerequisite subject. Combinatorial counting is reused by later probability review, rather than treating the absence of probability language in lesson 12 as a defect.

## Evidence, variants and deliberate limits

Cheap deterministic recognition and short typed answers dominate these additions. Exact term repetition is intentional. Authored variations change retrieval direction, graph structure, index dependency, order sensitivity, or the kind of growth comparison. The ten fixed cards target precise terminology, while twenty-one authored families address distinct misconceptions and computations. The catalog’s evidence labels, not the word “recall,” determine what is demonstrated.

Existing production/reasoning targets are not weakened. No proof is marked Quick merely to fill a column. A one-word deterministic answer does not certify a proof, and a choice about a characteristic equation does not replace producing its polynomial. The graph-growth objective remains open reasoning because its justification must connect representation, the counted operation and the graph theorem. The existing provider handles that occasional reasoning task; all other additions use current deterministic choice, term, exact, expression or graph grading.

Generator development is deferred. The reusable catalog already offers substantial numerical variation; the demonstrated gaps can be filled with bounded authored cases and existing validators. There is no runtime LLM question generation. Additional variants for singleton advanced proofs, forced/repeated-root recurrences, structural induction, graph-search subtleties, and fine-grained asymptotic definitions should be driven by actual delayed learner attempts. This audit supplies no learner-history calibration for these later lessons.

The large MIT PDF exceeded the web extractor's limit, so the original PDF was downloaded into ignored source research and the relevant passages were read locally. The scanned BFS analysis page was rendered and visually inspected. Sources support the mathematics, not a claim that they authored these original examples.

Conventions are explicit: a path in this curriculum has no repeated vertices, while Levin permits a repeated starting vertex; a rooted leaf means no children, including a singleton root; and empty-graph conventions remain those of the lesson. The citations record these differences rather than silently changing definitions.

Independent checks enumerate the small counting outcomes and all orders for the new DAGs; recurrence values and polynomial factorizations are checked separately. Twenty new fixture groups contain 82 accepted, rejected and malformed-input cases, exercised by the existing TypeScript content build and published Go fixture suite. Content/source validation and targeted Review-template/source tests pass. Targeted Go checks cover published fixtures, effective catalog endpoints, deterministic provider traps, and frozen/restored Review definitions. The root integration runs the complete CI-equivalent suite and final browser checks once all subject changes are combined.

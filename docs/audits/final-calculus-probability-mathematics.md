# Final Calculus and Probability & Statistics mathematical verification

Inspected 2026-09-20, independently from the original authoring passes, against `3719ac2` and the changes recorded here. This is the two-subject component of the final curriculum audit, not an audit of the other subjects or a browser/synchronization certification.

No incorrect numerical answer or invalid worked proof was found **under its intended assumptions**. The audit did find substantive missing assumptions, standalone prompts without their data, nonunique questions paired with single-answer grading, valid sampling names rejected by grading, an incomplete grading domain, and six damaged TeX strings. The repairs below preserve every lesson slug, question ID, template ID, variant ID, official numerical result, and evidence classification. No Review families were added.

## Coverage and method

All 44 instructional lessons and both reading-only introductions were read in learner order. Coverage includes 2,274 worksheet prompts/solutions (1,117 Calculus; 1,157 Probability & Statistics), 673 authored Review variants (339; 334), 88 quick checks including distractors and option feedback, and the associated glossary definitions. Each distinct calculation/proof/model was reasoned through independently; repeated parameter families were checked by their general derivation and their instantiated values. The 471 open worksheet/Review responses were inspected for what their reasoning actually establishes. Deterministic assessments and concept/skill/evidence mappings were inspected alongside their prompts.

This was a manual mathematical audit supported by independent computations, not a formal proof certificate for 3,035 individual assessment instances. The new executable oracles independently check 51 numerical answers/boundaries: 16 finite-probability answers by enumerating labeled outcomes, 10 certified bounds by exact cross-products or family-error sums, five path rates, and 20 composition/loss derivatives by direct expansion. These oracles do not use the grader to derive their answers. Other mathematical checks are recorded in the lesson table and findings. Existing fixture success alone was not accepted as evidence of mathematical correctness.

Open-response grading remains interpretive. Arbitrary equivalent expressions and all possible learner phrasings are not exhaustively covered. Figure descriptions and underlying mathematics were read with the lessons; this subtask does not claim a new visual inspection of every figure/frame. Integrated browser screenshots and cross-language adversarial tests are recorded by the coordinating audit.

### M2 — MEDIUM: a requested bound or sufficient parameter was not unique

A valid looser bound, larger sufficient sample size, or smaller per-test level could be rejected by an exact comparator. For example, 8 is an integer upper bound for `|x+3|` when `|x−3|<1`, n=41 satisfies the first Chebyshev sample-size guarantee, and 0.005 suffices for five tests with family bound 0.05. The old comparators respectively expected 7, 40, and 0.01.

The smallest correction is to request the sharp bound, named theorem's bound, smallest integer certified by the stated bound, or largest common test level. The comparator and intended answer remain unchanged. “Smallest certified by Chebyshev” deliberately does not claim the smallest sample size possible with additional distributional information.

### M3 — MEDIUM: necessary differentiability and sign assumptions were missing

- **Probability 01** (`probability-statistics-probability-models-events`), worksheet **28**: a weight twice that of three other outcomes does not say that these are all outcomes. An additional outcome changes normalization. The prompt now states that there are exactly four possible outcomes, making the probability `2/(2+1+1+1)=2/5`. Grader involved: yes.
- **Calculus 06** (`calculus-related-rates-approximation`), worksheet **24, 25**, Review **S3R2**: `d²=x²+y²` alone permits both signs of d. Therefore `d'=(xx'+yy')/d` is not determined by x,y and their rates without choosing d. The repaired prompt explicitly defines d as a nonnegative distance. These nonzero examples then select the positive square root. Grader involved: yes.
- **Calculus 17** (`calculus-gradients`), worksheet **32–41**, Review **S4R1–3, S5R3**, glossary **`calculus-directional-derivative`**: a gradient existing at one point does not imply a valid linear approximation or the dot-product formula in other directions. Set `f(0,0)=0` and `f(x,y)=ax+by+xy/(x²+y²)` elsewhere. Its partials at zero are a,b, while its value on y=x tends to 1/2, so the directional derivative can fail. Add differentiability at the point. Grader involved: yes.
- **Calculus 18** (`calculus-multivariable-chain-rule`), worksheet **2, 4, 6, 8, 10**, Review **S1R1**: the same counterexample invalidates a path chain rule justified only by two partial values. The prompt now requires the outer function and path to be differentiable. Grader involved: yes.
- **Calculus 19** (`calculus-multivariable-extrema`), Review **S3R2**: three second-partial values at a stationary point alone do not establish the second-order approximation used in the Hessian test. For a counterexample, set `f(0,0)=0` and `f(x,y)=−x²−3y²+10x²y²/(x²+y²)` elsewhere. The stated second partials at zero exist, but f(t,t)=t²>0 while f(t,0)=−t²<0. Add twice continuous differentiability; then D=12>0 and f_xx=−2 imply a local maximum. Grader involved: yes, a term comparator.

### M4 — MEDIUM: the grading domain omitted the upper support endpoint

**Probability 10**, `probability-statistics-normal-distributions-transformations`, worksheet **31** asks for the transformed CDF of `U~Uniform(0,2)`, Y=U², **on 0<y<4**. Independently, `P(U²≤y)=P(U≤√y)=√y/2`. The grader recorded only y>0. It therefore refused the equivalent `sqrt(y)*(4-y)/(2*(4-y))`, whose only added singularity is at the excluded endpoint 4.

Fix: record both y>0 and 4−y>0, with a minimal extra-valid fixture. The baseline TypeScript grader then accepts the equivalent form, refuses an in-domain hole from a canceled y−2 factor, and rejects √y as the wrong value. This is a content-domain repair, with no validator-source change or dependence on the separate grader branch.

Remaining intentional limitation: the symbolic engine is conservative rather than a complete interval theorem prover. The mathematically valid canceled y−5 form remains an input error because it does not infer that y−5 cannot vanish from these inequalities. A form introducing `sqrt((4-y)^2)` also remains unsupported by its symbolic identity rules. These are input errors, not incorrect mathematical verdicts. They are documented for the broader deterministic audit.

### M5 — MEDIUM: standard full sampling names were rejected

**Probability 14**, `probability-statistics-data-samples-study-design`, worksheet **33, 34, 35** and Review **S4R3** accepted only “cluster,” “systematic,” or “convenience.” Standard full answers ending in “sampling” were marked incorrect. Independently identify the design: sampling all units in selected groups is cluster sampling; every fiftieth record after a random start is systematic sampling; the first thirty nearby shoppers is convenience sampling.

The term authoring helper now permits explicit aliases, used only for these existing assessments. Extra-valid fixtures cover each affected assessment; different designs remain incorrect. No synonym inference or broad fuzzy matching was introduced. Grader involved: yes.

### M7 — MEDIUM: glossary formulas silently assumed common moments

**Probability 15**, glossary **`probability-statistics-shared-noise`**: independence of components in Xi=B+εi does not imply equal noise variances. The general result is `Var(B)+ΣVar(εi)/n²`; `Var(B)+τ²/n` requires the stated common variance τ². With n=2 and noise variances 1,9, the contribution is 10/4, not 1/2 or 9/2. The definition now states the common-variance condition.

**Probability 16**, glossary **`probability-statistics-concentration-guarantee`**: independence and common variance alone do not identify the common target μ in a sample-mean bound. The definition now explicitly assumes common mean μ as well. For example, Xi=i with variance zero cannot concentrate about one fixed common mean as n varies. These definitions now match the already-correct assumptions in their teaching sections. Grader involvement: no.

### M8 — LOW: specifying a Hessian's diagonal did not specify its off-diagonal entries

**Calculus 19**, `calculus-multivariable-extrema`, Review **S2R2** asked “For Hessian diagonal (2,6)” when the intended matrix was diagonal. A matrix with those diagonal entries and nonzero cross terms has a different quadratic form. The prompt now explicitly gives the diagonal matrix `H=diag(2,6)`. Grader involvement: yes.

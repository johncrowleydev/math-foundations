# Final prerequisite and progression audit: Discrete Mathematics and Linear Algebra

Reviewed 2026-09-20 against `3719ac2`, independently of Review activation. Scope: the reading order, explanations, worked examples, all 2,064 published questions and the 54 inline quick-check presentations across 27 instructional lessons, plus both introductions. Quick checks share promoted question identities and are counted only once in the question total.

**No sequencing concern or missing prerequisite support requiring a content change was found. No clear exercise difficulty cliff was found.** This conclusion assumes the stated entry preparation: ordinary algebra, functions and coordinate geometry through precalculus for Discrete Mathematics; algebra and coordinate geometry plus the preceding discrete language/proof habits for Linear Algebra. It does not assume a learner with no algebra background.

The study plan explicitly refreshes algebra, functions, exponents, logarithms, inequalities and summation notation where needed. That is different from silently promising to teach all precalculus. The following table records mathematical dependencies and how the text handles them. “Intentional prerequisite” includes material already established in the stated earlier lessons. “Local refresher exists” includes a first local explanation adequate for the task, even if a later lesson develops the topic further. Ordinary equation manipulation, arithmetic, roots and factoring are “acceptable common algebra.”

## Exercise pacing and intentional limits

The published main reading route has **253 inline question placements** in these subjects; the remaining **1,811** question identities are in optional Practice. Each lesson also displays two quick checks using promoted identities. Dense banks of six numerical variants are not six consecutive compulsory reading interruptions. The exercise-order inspection distinguished that optional repetition from a difficulty cliff in the instructional route.

For example, the six related arithmetic instances in early Linear Algebra are followed by witnesses, counterexamples, proofs or modeling interpretation. The proof lessons supply concrete templates before requesting transfer; recurrence and asymptotic proofs have summation/induction support already available. Sets and Relations necessarily contain some proof before the named proof unit, but locally explain arbitrary elements, two inclusions and definition-based arguments. Moving every proof after the formal methods lessons would break these useful earlier explanations without fixing a demonstrated gap.

The optional worksheet banks are substantial and may take more than one sitting. This is not evidence that all should be assigned in one session, nor a reason to equalize question or Review-template counts. No removal, bulk generation, new Precalculus subject or pacing rewrite was justified by this pass.

The six domain/label mismatches documented in the [mathematical report](final-discrete-linear-mathematics.md) were real explanation defects, not prerequisite gaps. Their repairs preserve the reading sequence. This audit does not claim experimental evidence of learner difficulty or retention, and it does not change Review activation or evidence-depth semantics.

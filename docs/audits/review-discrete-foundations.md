## Lesson-level findings

- **Sets:** All ten taught concepts have reusable review. `set-operations × compute` already has ten production templates; `set-proofs × prove` has thirteen reasoning templates. Equality by double inclusion, identities, counterexamples, power sets, Cartesian products, partitions, inclusion–exclusion and indexed families have existing productive/deep coverage. Add exact retrieval for element, subset, proper subset, union, intersection, difference, complement, universe, empty set, cardinality, power set, Cartesian product, disjoint and partition. A four-variant recognition family isolates union, intersection, directional difference and complement with an explicit universe. It has its own objective and cannot satisfy computation or proof targets.
- **Relations:** All ten taught concepts have reusable review. Relation properties already have five recognition, five counterexample and five proof templates. Equivalence classes have four construction and three proof templates; equivalence relations have three proofs. Orders, extrema, Hasse diagrams, composition and closures also have eligible exercise representations. Add retrieval of relation, reflexive, symmetric, antisymmetric, transitive, equivalence relation, equivalence class and partial order. Three concrete relations test both symmetry/antisymmetry, neither property, and antisymmetry without symmetry. The instances change pair structure and the correct classification; they do not merely rename objects.
- **Functions:** Domain/codomain/range meaning was tested incidentally but exact vocabulary was absent. Add ten terms: function, domain, codomain, range/image, injective, surjective, bijective, inverse function, composition and preimage. Existing image computation (eight templates), image proofs (six), inverse construction (five), composition computation (five) and composition proofs (four) remain the application pool. The three square-function classification variants change the domain or codomain, so identical formulas require different conclusions. Injectivity/surjectivity proof targets also recur in the later proof lessons; adding duplicates in Lesson 5 is unnecessary.
- **Sequences and summations:** All thirteen concepts have existing review, but only three of 78 representations were Quick-compatible. Recurrence evaluation already has three production templates, while explicit-form verification has reasoning coverage. Add recursive definition, recurrence relation, initial conditions, arithmetic sequence and geometric sequence terminology at this first teaching location; Lesson 13 reuses these concepts instead of duplicating the terms. A three-variant exact-response family isolates inclusive endpoint counts, a singleton index and an empty index set. Reindexing, telescoping, nested sums, factorials, sum bounds and closed-form derivations retain their existing productive/reasoning tasks.
- **Direct proof:** The umbrella `direct-proof` concept itself had no effective row, although direct proof construction is extensively represented under parity (ten proofs), divisibility (seven), rationality (five), set proofs (eight), cases, biconditionals and constructive existence. Add the method's name as a recall objective, activated by existing `proof-obligations`, `parity` or `divisibility` evidence. Do not add a redundant umbrella proof. Witness and counterexample terminology is already covered by Lessons 1–2; nine counterexample-production representations and six constructive-existence representations here give application evidence.
- **Contrapositive:** Eleven transformation templates, one construction template, 34 contrapositive proofs and eight biconditional proofs already provide varied productive/deep practice. The logical form of the contrapositive also has dedicated review from Lesson 1. Add only recall of the proof method from its assumption/goal structure, under `contrapositive-proof`; do not duplicate the earlier logical-form objective.
- **Contradiction:** Eight target-negation transformations and twelve contradiction proofs coexist with rationality, infinitude, uniqueness and proof-diagnosis work. Add the method name and a three-variant recognition family that selects the negation of a conditional, a universal statement, or nonexistence of a greatest integer. This cheap setup probe cannot clear the existing transformation/proof targets. Lowest-terms interpretation already has two Quick templates plus a recognition template, so no extra terminology card is needed solely to increase counts.

## Activation, evidence and deliberate limits

Each new recall objective is attached to existing primary exercise concepts, not reading or scrolling. The direct-proof exception is explicit above: its own concept lacks exercise evidence, so its activation uses the actual concepts exercised while learning the method. Every selected activation concept has an existing eligible lesson-exercise representation. Merely requesting catalog entries or previews creates no learning evidence.

Term retrieval supplies **production** evidence because the answer is entered without choices. Its low cost and `short-text` input legitimately permit Quick. Each term has its own recall objective; retrieving “contrapositive proof” is not evidence of constructing a contrapositive or writing a proof. The four recognition families also have separate objectives, preserving every original target's required depth. The one-number summation family supplies production evidence only for its narrow counting objective.

Forty fixed prompts are intentional: exact repetition is useful when the target is the name of a precise mathematical definition. Accepted aliases include taught names such as one-to-one/onto and range/image. Longer definitions and open term grading are unnecessary. Authored variation is concentrated where changing a set operation, relation structure, domain/codomain or logical target changes the actual retrieval work.

Single-template tasks such as constructing a partition, representing a set, constructing a sequence, diagnosing reindexing or explaining recurrence uniqueness remain available. The surrounding concepts have other application forms, and there is no learner history showing that repeating these particular tasks is harmful. Expanding every such cell would produce a quota-driven deck. Proofs, compound symbolic transforms, longer sum calculations and graphical Hasse work intentionally retain Regular-only representations. No new proof/reasoning content or cumulative items are needed within this sub-scope; the curriculum-wide pass evaluates bridges separately.

Normal function, set, summation and proof review supplies the Discrete → Linear Algebra boundary. This pass adds no prerequisite curriculum or standalone algebra material. Glossary/definition expansion for less central terms such as singleton, restriction, floor and ceiling, or every order extremum is deferred: existing notation/application questions already test them, and future sessions should identify which exact names need separate recall.

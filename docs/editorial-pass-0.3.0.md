# Editorial and interaction pass for 0.3.0

The pass covers all 15 lessons and the displayed instructions and prompts for all 1,313 original exercises. Every original ID, formula field, truth-table definition, and handwriting page remains intact. Thirty additional quick checks provide optional short practice; none replaces a written exercise.

Lesson prose now explains the meaning and use of definitions, connects proof steps, and develops concrete examples. Lesson 1 includes both distributive laws, reverse factoring, absorption, three worked simplifications with reasons for each step, and explanations of every listed inference rule. Historical notes on ancient logic and Euler's bridges link to their sources. Lesson endings direct readers to native practice rather than printed downloads.

Each exercise now has a complete app-specific copy record. The former shared directions are not consulted at runtime or during content generation. Context is retained where it supplies an actual domain, graph, recurrence, or convention; generic repeated commands are removed. Notable corrections include:

- Explicit predicates and domains without asking early predicate questions for a witness.
- Both missing permission-set definitions in the policy exercises.
- Named properties in function and relation classification questions.
- Integer ranges and recurrence starting indices on individual cards.
- The menu, graph, tree, oscillating function, or recurrence formerly referenced as “that” object.
- A complete statement of the horses induction fallacy and precise missing-range questions.
- The distinction between a prime divisor and a necessarily prime product-plus-one construction.

All 121 inline handwriting placements retain their prerequisite traces. Updated prompts, instructions, answers, and teaching paragraphs were compared with those traces before refreshing the audit. Audit text is now a checked snapshot, not an override that can silently hide changes to the canonical exercise copy. Quick-check wording, choices, and explanations were checked against the teaching before their named anchors; the build validates their anchor hashes, choices, and math.

Quick checks present neutral selections and a separate answer reveal, without scoring or blocking progress. Choices and reveal state survive rotation and leaving the lesson. Two-finger flicks use Android's decelerating scroll physics, stop at content boundaries, and are interrupted by new contact. Pausing before lifting, or leaving one finger down, must not release stale momentum.
